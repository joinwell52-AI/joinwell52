# A Lost Acknowledgement Leaves the Operation Unresolved

An order is submitted, the connection breaks, and only a pending local record remains. Submitting again may duplicate the order; clearing the record may abandon responsibility for an effect already produced. This is an illustrative failure scenario. A timeout does not settle the remote outcome. Even a well-formed recovered record must first be tied to the operation and then interpreted by a recovery policy. The practical result is a disposition table separating retention, revalidation and terminal clearing.

## The gap between local commit and remote effect

Provenance-Isolated Epistemic Quorums (PIEQ) distinguishes an effect sharing an atomic boundary with its deduplication record from an operation genuinely executed elsewhere. The former can coordinate the decision within that boundary. Without a shared transaction, a lost acknowledgement from the latter does not let the caller infer that nothing happened. The paper requires retaining an unknown outcome and reconciling it, rather than promoting local deduplication into exactly-once remote effect. [Paper, section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32032v1)

The pinned controller releases exposure only after verified fenced no-effect; otherwise it marks the outcome unknown. This observation does not establish crash durability of the in-memory ledger. [Controller source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openkedge/openkedge/80836b200ec56d8d4ef845cf86c0c2873314b287/research/pieq/pieq/hardened.py)

This boundary does not imply a universal ban on retries. A verified target-native idempotency contract can safely coalesce some repeated requests. Its operation identity, retention window and failure boundary matter. Those target guarantees were not tested here, so they do not authorize declaring a particular remote call safe to resubmit.

## Recovering an order requires recovering the same order

The independently authored Vibe-Trading project supplies a concrete reconciliation path. Its paper separates research evidence from submission authority and distinguishes accepted, rejected and unresolved orders. The inspected implementation persists an Alpaca operation intent before submission. Its paper and repository remain one source actor. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24845v1)

The pinned recovery function queries the original client order ID rather than submitting again. Its validator requires a broker order ID and compares client ID, symbol, side, order type and time in force, then quantity, notional and limit price. The timestamp must include a timezone, fill quantity must be nonnegative, and status must belong to a supported set. These checks concern consistency with the pending request; they do not independently authenticate the provider’s truthfulness. [Order recovery source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading/2134f408b1cd715c74fa8c5021f4402d50b00ea8/agent/src/live/sdk_order_gate.py)

The pending-action module also binds request, client ID, broker ID, phase and position attribution. That module supports the Alpaca path. It cannot establish equivalent recovery behavior for every connector. [Pending-action source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/Vibe-Trading/2134f408b1cd715c74fa8c5021f4402d50b00ea8/agent/src/live/pending_action.py)

Independence here means independent authorship: one team supplies the atomic-boundary and retained-responsibility argument; another supplies an order implementation. That supports mechanism comparison, not mutual experimental reproduction, live reliability or investment performance.

## Why filled with zero fill can pass one check

The same-run Analysis stage executed two sets of internal isolated probes: seventeen record-validation cases and five recovery cases. Original functions were extracted from pinned source and run with synthetic requests, broker replies and boundary doubles. Counting, durable audit, pending transitions and clearing recorded requested calls instead of implementing real storage. Production consumes those completed experiments without rerunning them.

The validator rejected identity or size mismatches, timezone-free timestamps, unknown status and malformed numbers; numeric 10 and 10.00 compared equal. A deliberately inconsistent reply said filled while reporting zero fill. The record validator returned normalized evidence. The subsequent recovery function detected the status/fill contradiction and took its halt branch. Reading the first acceptance as system-level permission would misidentify the decision layer.

| Synthetic input | Observed layer | Actual result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client ID or quantity mismatch | Record validator | No usable recovery record |
| Lookup exception or mismatched identity | Recovery function | Unresolved refusal; no resubmission |
| Identity-consistent working order | Recovery function | Requests count accounting and policy revalidation |
| Identity-consistent rejected order with zero fill | Recovery function | Requests clearing the pending record |
| Filled status with zero fill | Validator, then recovery | First accepted, subsequently halted |

[Complete internal probe cases and results](/assets/evidence/2026-11-26-sig-20261126-003/experiment-evidence.json)

This is why the layers matter. Record structure and field consistency supply interpretable material; recovery policy decides its implications. None of the five recovery cases called order submission, but that does not establish the behavior of every upstream entry point or durable completion of clearing recorded by a double.

## Keep responsibility attached to the evidence

The following is a proposed responsibility table for remote writes, not a description of behavior already shared by all trading systems. Waiting, refusing mismatched evidence and halting on contradictions remain distinct outcomes.

| Responsible actor and input | Artifact to retain | Disposition boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Submitter constructs intent | Identity and request tied to one operation | Distinguish unsent from sent but unacknowledged |
| Recovery owner receives timeout | Original pending record and lookup attempt | Read failure leaves the outcome unresolved |
| Validator receives external record | Consistency result and normalized evidence | Do not apply another operation’s record |
| Recovery policy receives working status | Ownership and revalidation requirement | Still working does not renew write authority |
| Clearing owner receives exact terminal outcome | Operation-specific outcome and clearing result | Failed clearing retains recovery responsibility |

In the inspected path, rejection and working status produce different dispositions. Partial fills introduce position attribution and cannot inherit a zero-fill clearing rule. Review persistence of the pending record, daily count, audit and external order separately. A clearing call in code does not make clearing atomic with the broker effect.

## What the next fault injection must answer

These observations are version-bounded: order recovery comes from commit 2134f408b1cd715c74fa8c5021f4402d50b00ea8; the atomic-boundary comparison uses commit 80836b200ec56d8d4ef845cf86c0c2873314b287 and paper v1. The former is later implementation evidence, not the paper evaluation’s frozen snapshot. The probes do not reproduce a live broker, durable file locking, audit failures, partial-fill attribution or competing external clients.

PIEQ’s physical tests also remain author reports. A shared host kernel and a 1 Hz observer cannot exclude shorter dual-primary intervals; native cluster protections cannot be attributed to logical reasoning. Publication and public source establish provenance without supplying independent validation. [Atomic boundary and physical-test discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.32032v1)

Next, an authorized test environment should inject a well-formed but stale external record, failed audit persistence, interrupted clearing, restart after partial fill and concurrent action by another client. Target-native idempotency must also be tested. Until these questions have answers, retaining the pending operation for manual reconciliation may be appropriate. Recovery earns its value first by preserving responsibility, not by declaring completion faster.

INDUSTRY ARCHITECTURE

Industry Architecture

Selected
Q-20260918-01 · Shared multi-agent coordination rules need pre-execution robustness guarantees under stochastic rewards

Selected from SIG-20260918-007 after Skill 02 triage. Social Laws supplies a bounded architecture question that remains meaningful without the paper name: a coordination rule that appears acceptable in deterministic demonstrations may still impose unacceptable outcomes when execution is stochastic and each agent optimizes its own reward. The primary study defines alpha-robustness as a minimum retained-utility guarantee under a shared social law and reduces verification to a series of Markov decision processes. The Industry Architecture question is therefore how admissible interaction constraints should be specified and verified before execution so coordination safety is not inferred from observed success alone. This is distinct from the September 14 CoCoBench object, which decomposed coordination competence into allocation, ordering, mutual exclusion and handoff for measurement, and from the September 11 UnitBoost object, which asked when deterministic operators can replace generative managers. Today's object studies ex-ante robustness guarantees for a shared coordination policy under stochastic outcomes.

OPEN SOURCE ENGINEERING

Open-source Engineering

Selected
Q-20260918-02 · Long-running tool execution should expose bounded progress telemetry instead of forcing the runtime to guess

Selected from SIG-20260918-006 as the strongest Open-source Engineering object. The study isolates a runtime observability problem: pre-call duration estimates are inherently stale at the moment a long-running tool is actually progressing, while the tool execution layer may already know remaining work or near-completion state. Its harness exposes bounded progress signals outside the agent-visible semantic context and reports roughly 20.7% and 20.8% reductions in p90 post-tool-call TTFT versus LRU in the reported HBM-only and HBM-plus-DRAM settings. The bounded engineering question is what machine-observable progress contract the execution layer should publish to scheduling and recovery logic, how much decision value it adds, and how to keep operational telemetry separate from business semantics. This is distinct from the September 16 Skill Routing object, which studied how to choose capabilities as catalogs grow, and from long-horizon completion research, which asks whether required business actions were actually completed; today's object studies runtime telemetry while a tool is still executing.
